QUADmapper Quick Start

Put an image or video on a quad, warp it to fit a surface, and send it to the projector. About two minutes to read.

On a Mac: this guide names the Windows keys. Press Cmd where it says Ctrl and Option where it says Alt. Two shortcuts differ: perform mode is Option+P (not F11) and mesh edit is Option+M (not Ctrl+M). The menus show the Mac keys.

1. The window

QUADmapper opens with a quad already on the canvas, so you can start at once.

  • Canvas: the large area in the middle. You see and edit your quads here.
  • Quad List: every quad, with tick boxes to show, hide and lock it. Use + Add Quad, Duplicate and Delete here.
  • Properties: the settings of whatever is selected: corners, mesh, crop, colour, and the settings of a selected output.
  • Resources: a window with tabs. The Media tab holds your files, the Scenes tab your scenes and the Outputs tab your projectors. In Advanced mode it also has the Project and Preview tabs.
  • Menus: File, Edit, Window, Output, AI and Help. A status bar along the bottom of the window shows messages.

Simple and Advanced mode

The Simple and Advanced buttons in the menu bar switch modes. Simple mode shows only what you need for a first mapping, plus the Apply Test Grid and Send to Projector (F11) buttons. In both modes, Test Grid: On in the menu bar puts the test grid on every new quad, the first one included; click it to turn that off. Quads you already have, and the quads of a show you open, keep what they show. Advanced mode adds slices, calibration and the other tabs. QUADmapper starts in the mode you used last. Your panel layout is remembered between runs; Window > Reset Layout restores the default.

2. Your first mapping

Load a picture or video, pull the quad's corners onto your surface, then send it to the projector.

Load media

  1. Open the Media tab. Click Load Files... for single files, or Load Folder... for a whole folder.
  2. Drag a thumbnail from the tab onto the quad on the canvas.

For a video you can also use the Video Player panel (open it from the Window menu): select the quad, then click Load Video....

Warp it

  1. Click a corner of the quad, or press Tab to select a corner. Shift+Tab goes the other way round.
  2. Press the arrow keys to nudge the corner. Hold Shift to move 10 times as far. Hold Alt to move a tenth as far, for fine adjustment.
  3. You can also drag a corner with the mouse.
  4. Repeat for each corner until the picture sits on your surface.
The arrow keys and Tab belong to the canvas while a quad is selected. Ctrl+Z undoes a move.
A quad whose lock tick box is on in the Quad List cannot be reshaped or deleted until you unlock it.

Linear or Perspective warp

Properties has a Warp choice for each quad.

  • Linear (new quads) spaces the picture evenly along every edge. Two quads snapped together line up across the join, for example the two walls of a corner.
  • Perspective keeps lines perfectly straight inside a strongly tilted quad. Choose it for a single flat surface seen at an angle. Projects saved before this choice existed open in Perspective.

Send it to the projector

  1. Press F11, or click Send to Projector (F11) in Simple mode. The same command is in the menu as Output > Perform Mode.
  2. The picture goes fullscreen on the projector. With no projector connected, it fills this screen instead, so you can check it before the show.
  3. Press Esc or F11 to come back to the editor.
QUADmapper finds the screens and projectors plugged into this computer by itself: each one gets an output at its own resolution and a scene of its own. To change which screen an output uses, use the Outputs tab (see Outputs).

3. Finer warping

Four corners fix a flat surface. For a curved surface, turn on the quad's grid warp and move its points.

  1. Select the quad and tick Grid warp in Properties, in Simple or Advanced mode, or press Ctrl+M (Toggle Mesh Edit). The grid's points appear on the canvas, with the curve handles of the selected point.
  2. Drag a point, or use Tab and the arrow keys as before. Drag a grid line to move the whole line.
  3. For more points, click Add Column or Add Row: each puts a line in the middle of the widest column or row. To place a line yourself, click Click-add Column or Click-add Row, then press on the quad, or on its edge (the top or bottom edge for a column, a side for a row), and drag the line into place. Alt+= and Alt+- give an even grid one step finer or coarser.
  4. Points are Spline (the grid curves smoothly through them) or Linear (the grid runs straight between them). The Spline / Linear choice beside Click-add sets the type of the lines you add from now on. To change one point, right-click it on the canvas; All points changes them all. On a Perspective quad, Linear lines bend a little with the perspective; use the Linear warp for dead-straight lines.
  5. Image: Warp bends the picture with the grid. Image: Crop keeps the picture flat on the four corners and uses the bent outline, and curved edges, to cut a shape out of it; outside the picture is transparent.
  6. Reset Mesh (Alt+R) puts every point back on the plain corner-pin shape and keeps the grid lines.
Crop in Properties (the source region) can be set in % of the picture or in px, its pixels. Pixels need a video or image on the quad.

4. Scenes and takes

A scene is one full arrangement of quads. A take puts a scene on air.

  1. Use the Scenes tab to add, duplicate, rename and reorder scenes. Click a scene to work on it; the canvas and the Quad List then show its quads.
  2. With several projectors, each one starts with its own scene: Scene 1 on Projector 1, Scene 2 on Projector 2, and so on. Click between the scenes to work on one projector, then another. To change which output shows a scene, pick it in the scene's row.
  3. Two projectors stacked on the same surface: click Other scenes: On on the canvas toolbar. The other scenes' quads show as orange outlines, and with Snap: On a corner or mesh point you drag snaps to them. Outlines are placed by pixel from the top-left of the canvas, so they line up for projectors of the same resolution.
  4. Press 1 to 9 to put that scene on air. The keys work in the editor and on a focused projector window, whatever your keyboard layout.
  5. For a crossfade, set Crossfade (seconds) in Edit > Preferences... (General tab). 0 is a cut.

Compositions: a timeline or a switcher on one quad

A composition plays several clips, images or generators on a quad, one after another, with a crossfade between them. A Timeline moves on by itself after each cue's hold time. A Switcher moves on only when told to: from the panel, by OSC or by MIDI.

  1. Open the Compositions tab in the Resources window and click New Timeline or New Switcher.
  2. Add cues with Add cue from the media bin..., or drag media from the bin onto it. A video holds for its own length to begin with; change the Hold time per cue (0 waits for Next).
  3. Set the Crossfade time (0 is a cut). With Restart clips on cue on, each video starts from its beginning when its cue comes on.
  4. Select quads on the canvas and click Put on N selected quads.
  5. Use Previous, Next and the numbered cue buttons during the show. They work while the show is locked.

A composition's sound is the sound of the cue showing. Video export renders compositions but does not yet include their sound.

5. Outputs

An output is one projector, or one window on a screen. The Outputs and Scenes tabs are there in both modes; slices need Advanced mode.

When QUADmapper starts, and on File > New Project, every screen gets an output named Projector 1, Projector 2 and so on, at that screen's resolution, each with a scene of its own. The screen the editor is on comes last; in perform mode its output covers the editor (Esc or F11 brings the editor back). Untick that output in the Outputs tab to keep the editor in view. A projector plugged in while QUADmapper runs gets one too, and the status bar says so. With only one screen, F11 shows Projector 1 on it. That waits while you are in perform mode or the show is locked. A saved show opens with its own outputs.

  1. Open the Outputs tab in the Resources window.
  2. Click Identify. For 5 seconds each projector shows its output's number, the same number as in the list.
  3. To change an output's screen, select it and choose one in the Monitor list under the outputs. The output takes that screen's resolution. Set its rotation if the projector is on its side.
  4. For another window on a screen, click Add Output.
  5. In the Scenes tab, choose which output shows each scene.

An output can show slices of the scene, each with its own warp and edge blending where two projectors overlap. Select the output to edit them in Properties.

Export a video for a media player

To play the show without the PC, render it to video files: one MP4 (H.264, with the videos' sound) per output, at the output's resolution and rotation.

  1. Choose File > Export Video....
  2. Tick the outputs to export. Each one gets its own file, named after it.
  3. Set the length. When only one video is mapped, The video's length is offered; otherwise type the seconds.
  4. Pick the frame rate and whether to include sound, then choose a folder and click Export.

The export renders every frame, however busy the PC is, and the projectors show it as it goes. You can keep editing; changes made meanwhile are not in the file, except a scene take, so take scenes before you export. Cancel stops it and leaves no file. Export is off while the show is locked. Sound is heard only for videos playing forward at 1x speed, as in the live show.

6. During the show

  • Blackout: Ctrl+B turns every projector black. Press it again to bring the picture back. It is also in the Output menu.
  • Perform Lock: Ctrl+Shift+L (or Output > Perform Lock) stops accidental edits. Locking is immediate. To unlock, QUADmapper asks Unlock the Show? and you confirm.

What the lock blocks

Editing quads on the canvas, the Edit commands (including undo and delete), dropping media, New and Open, importing 3D objects, calibration and slice editing, AI assist, MIDI learn and Preferences.

What keeps working

Scene takes and fades, blackout, F11 and Esc, OSC and MIDI control, the Video Player transport, saving, canvas zoom and pan, and Exit.

7. Saving, autosave and recovery

  • File > Save Project (Ctrl+S) saves. Save Project As... (Ctrl+Shift+S) saves under a new name. Projects are .qmap files. File > Open Recent lists the last ten.
  • QUADmapper also saves unsaved work to a recovery file, every minute by default. Change this in Edit > Preferences... under Auto-save.
  • After a crash, QUADmapper opens a Recover Unsaved Work window the next time. Click Restore to get the work back, or Discard to drop it.
  • If a project's media files have moved, use File > Relocate Missing Media.... Missing media shows black on the projectors.
  • Open projects only from people you trust. A project can point at media on a network share (\\computer\share\...), and opening it makes Windows connect to that computer and sign in with your Windows account.

9. Remote control by OSC and MIDI

A lighting desk, a tablet or a MIDI controller can take scenes, trigger blackout and move controls.

  1. Open Window > Control (OSC / MIDI).
  2. For OSC, tick Enable OSC input. QUADmapper listens on the UDP port shown there (8000 to begin with). Change the port, then click Apply. OSC is off until you turn it on.
  3. For MIDI, tick your device in the MIDI list, then use MIDI Learn to bind a control to a target.

For example, /qm/blackout toggles blackout, and /qm/scene/take takes a scene. A composition is addressed by its name: /qm/composition/Timeline 1/next, /prev, /cue with the cue number, /play, /pause, /restart and /fade with the crossfade in seconds (up to 30). A short name without spaces is easiest to type on a controller.

10. Where to get help

The Help menu has User Guide, AI Features Guide, Keyboard Shortcuts, Open Log Folder, Report a Bug, Open-Source Licences and About QUADmapper. If you report a bug, attach the log from Open Log Folder. The AI features are a beta, off until you tick Edit > Preferences... > General > AI Assist (beta); that adds the AI (Beta) menu and the AI Features Guide item. They have their own guide, ai-features-guide.html.
